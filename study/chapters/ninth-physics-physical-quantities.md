# Physical Quantities & Measurement

## What is a Physical Quantity?

A **physical quantity** is anything that can be **measured**. Every physical quantity has:
1. A **numerical value** (how much)
2. A **unit** (what it is measured in)

For example: mass = **5 kg**, length = **3 metres**, time = **10 seconds**

---

## Base Quantities

The 7 fundamental (base) quantities in the SI system:

| Base Quantity | SI Unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Length | metre | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Electric Current | ampere | A |
| Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Luminous intensity | candela | cd |

> **Tip:** Learn these 7 — they come up in every paper!

---

## Derived Quantities

Derived quantities are made by combining base quantities.

| Derived Quantity | Formula | SI Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | length / time | m/s |
| Force | mass × acceleration | kg·m/s² = Newton (N) |
| Work / Energy | force × distance | N·m = Joule (J) |
| Power | work / time | J/s = Watt (W) |
| Pressure | force / area | N/m² = Pascal (Pa) |

---

## Scientific Notation

Very large or very small numbers are written in scientific notation:

$$N = M \times 10^n$$

where $1 \leq M < 10$ and $n$ is an integer.

**Examples:**
- Speed of light: $3 \times 10^8$ m/s
- Size of an atom: $1 \times 10^{-10}$ m
- Mass of electron: $9.1 \times 10^{-31}$ kg

## Significant Figures

Significant figures show how **precise** a measurement is.

Rules:
- All non-zero digits are significant: **345** → 3 sig figs
- Zeros between non-zero digits are significant: **3005** → 4 sig figs
- Leading zeros are NOT significant: **0.005** → 1 sig fig
- Trailing zeros after a decimal point ARE significant: **3.50** → 3 sig figs

## Types of Errors

### Random Error
Caused by **unpredictable** changes in conditions. Reduced by taking multiple readings and averaging.

$$\text{Mean value} = \frac{x_1 + x_2 + \cdots + x_n}{n}$$

### Systematic Error
Caused by a **faulty instrument** or poor technique. The error is always in the same direction. Cannot be reduced by repeating the experiment — the instrument must be fixed or replaced.

### Percentage Error

$$\text{Percentage Error} = \frac{\text{Absolute Error}}{\text{Measured Value}} \times 100\%$$

---

## Important MCQ Tips

- The **metre** is the SI unit of length, not centimetre
- **Kilogram** is a base unit — gram is NOT
- Speed is a **scalar**, velocity is a **vector** (same formula, different nature)
- When adding measurements, the result has the same number of **decimal places** as the least precise measurement
